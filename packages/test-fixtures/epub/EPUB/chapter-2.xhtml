Finding an Anchor

An anchor is useful because it is stable, not because it is loud. In focused reading, the first part of a word can be emphasized while the remaining letters keep their natural weight.

Typography is only one part of comfort. Font size, line height, word spacing, margins, contrast, and the choice between pages and continuous scrolling all affect the reading experience.

Preferences must survive a change of platform. A book opened on the web should feel familiar when it is opened in the desktop application, even before cross-device synchronization exists.

Local-first software makes a simple promise: the book and its learning history remain available without a network connection. Online translation and web search may help, but they should be optional doors rather than foundations.

At the end of this experiment, the reader should be able to move backward and forward, switch layout modes, change the text presentation, select a phrase, and serialize a useful location.
